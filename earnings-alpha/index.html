Does segment-level sentiment beat whole-transcript sentiment?

A falsifiable test on 16 real, first-party earnings-call transcripts: whether the dispersion of FinBERT sentiment across a call's Q&A segments predicts abnormal returns beyond what the whole-transcript average already explains.

n = 16 calls 5 tickers · 4 sectors BAC, CAT, JPM, MSFT, XOM FinBERT · Fama-French · PSR
01 · Hypothesis

A falsifiable claim, stated before looking at results

H1: the dispersion of sentiment across a call's segments (max − min segment sentiment) has incremental predictive power for the 1-day and 3-day abnormal return around the call, beyond what whole-transcript sentiment alone explains.

Why this, not another metric

Most quant portfolios show price-only signals; most IB-style analyses show no statistical validation. This sits between the two — a qualitative artifact (an earnings call) tested with a quant-style falsifiable claim, explicit backtest, and transaction costs. A null result here is treated as a complete, reportable finding, not a failed run.

02 · Method

Six steps, each independently auditable

1 · Segment each call into prepared-remarks blocks and analyst Q&A exchanges (rule-based, not learned). 2 · Score each segment with FinBERT. 3 · Compute whole-transcript sentiment, segment dispersion, and core-segment sentiment. 4 · Measure Fama-French market-model abnormal returns for [0,+1] and [0,+3] trading days. 5 · Test with a nested regression isolating dispersion's incremental R². 6 · Backtest a walk-forward long/short sort, scored with the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio.

07 · Limitations, stated up front

What this is not

01

Small, non-random sample. 16 calls across 5 tickers, curated for transcript availability — not a random draw from the market.

02

No multiple-testing correction across the two return windows tested (1-day, 3-day).

03

Core-segment keywords use present-day knowledge of each firm's largest business line, which may not match an older quarter.

04

Daily closes, not intraday. Event-window returns are coarser than sub-minute market-microstructure studies.

05

Research prototype, not a claim of a deployable trading signal.
