I build the infrastructure that runs my own AI assistants.

Self-hosted, automated end to end, and documented well enough that I can rebuild the whole thing from scratch.

What I build

A small, private platform that hosts a set of AI agents and the services they depend on. It runs on a single rented server, entirely under my own control: containerised services, a private network with no directly exposed ports, an encrypted secrets workflow, and a database and cache layer the agents share.

On top of that sits an automation layer — workflows that pick up events, route them to the right agent, and deliver the results back to me on whatever channel I happen to be using. The agents cover the areas of my life that generate recurring work: university, my job, the job search, and personal projects.

The unglamorous half matters just as much: scheduled encrypted backups with restore drills, health checks and alerting, configuration management so the machine can be re-provisioned from code, and continuous integration that refuses to let a broken change reach the server.

Approach

When two options conflict, I decide in a fixed order. It removes most of the arguing with myself:

  1. Security — the default answer is the closed one.
  2. Maintainability — I will be the one reading this in a year.
  3. Simplicity — fewer moving parts beats a clever one.
  4. Automation — anything done twice by hand gets written down as code.
  5. Reproducibility — if it only exists on one machine, it does not exist.

Every decision of consequence gets a short written record: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why. Several of those records exist mainly because I later proved myself wrong and wanted the correction on file.
